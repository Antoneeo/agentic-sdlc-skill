# Replay record — kb recall, second instrument (2026-09-12)

**What was claimed, before the probe.** The recall's four verdicts all sit downstream
of the descent, with no second instrument: a descent that picks the wrong branch
yields `kb: no coverage`, indistinguishable from genuine absence, after which the
agent answers from model memory. Claimed severity: high, frequency unmeasured.

**How it was probed.** Two subject runs, each a fresh agent with no other context,
given the current recall doctrine verbatim (`SKILL.md` §Topic Recall + `taxonomy.md`
§1/§6), a fixture KB by path, and one practitioner question. Neither subject was told
what outcome was expected, and neither was told a defect was under test. Both were
asked to report the declared verdict, the answer, and every file opened and search run.

## Run 1 — vocabulary mismatch (fixture_kb)

One topic, `deployment`, described "how releases reach production, and who approves
them". The question asked whether rollback is manual or automatic: the terms
`rollback` and `canary` appear in the claim rows and in neither the description nor
the synonyms.

- **Verdict returned:** `kb: deployment → 3 claims cited`.
- **Outcome: the claim was found.** The subject cited `c-dp-03` as current and named
  `c-dp-02`'s supersession, per the doctrine's own rule.
- **Why the fixture proved nothing.** The descent is semantic coverage judgement over
  *concepts*, not term matching over strings. A description that summarizes the topic
  still covers questions about the claims inside it — which is the design working, not
  failing. The measurement `the index row does not contain the question's term` is
  therefore about vocabulary, never about reachability.

## Run 2 — wrong-branch risk (fixture_kb2)

Eight topics with realistic descriptions. The answering claim — access-log retention,
90 days, a DPO ruling — sits under `gdpr_compliance` ("lawful basis, data-subject
rights and the DPO approval chain"). The obvious branch for a question about logs is
`observability` ("logging, metrics, tracing and the alerting rules", synonym `log`),
which carries log claims but nothing on retention, and whose `gaps:` does not mention
retention either. No topic's index row contains any of `retention`, `retain`, `conserv`
or `90`.

- **Verdict returned:** `kb: gdpr_compliance, observability → 2 claims cited`.
- **Outcome: the claim was found.** The subject opened three candidate branches
  (`observability` via the synonym, `gdpr_compliance`, `identity`), cited `c-gdpr-01`
  as the answer, and distinguished the ruling's authority from the elicited
  collateral claim.
- **The subject ran a text search unprompted** — action 7 of its record:
  `grep -rniE "retain|retention|conserv|SUPERSEDED|log" topics/` — as a cross-check
  for a duplicate or contradicting claim. Nothing in the doctrine asked for it.

## What the probe established

1. **The false-negative claim is not reproduced.** Two fixtures, one built
   specifically for wrong-branch risk with a deliberately more plausible decoy branch,
   both returned the right claim. The failure the remedy was designed to prevent did
   not occur.
2. **The descent is multi-branch by mandate, and that is what carried both runs.**
   `taxonomy.md` §1 requires following every candidate and opening the final
   candidates — plural. A single-path descent was never the doctrine.
3. **The remedy is already emergent.** The text cross-check appeared without being
   mandated, on the corpus property the Vision already declares (file-based,
   *greppabile*). Mandating it would add doctrine bytes for a behaviour a capable
   subject performs unprompted.
4. **One defect WAS reproduced, and it is not the one under test.** The four legal
   verdicts assume exactly one matched slug, while the doctrine mandates descending
   every candidate branch. Run 2 had to invent a form — `kb: gdpr_compliance,
   observability → 2 claims cited` — and said so explicitly: "the slug slot carries
   two slugs because the descent legitimately matched two branches". A mandated act
   with no legal way to declare its result is a contract gap, found by executing the
   text rather than reading it.

## Standing limits of this record

Two runs, one subject model, fixtures authored by the same session that made the
claim. A negative result on two fixtures bounds the defect's frequency; it does not
prove the absence of a shape that would reproduce it. What it does settle is that the
claim did not hold on the two shapes offered as its evidence, including the one built
to be hostile.
