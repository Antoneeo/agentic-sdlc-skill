# Elicitation — question waves

Ask for the **benefit** the user wants, never for the mechanism they imagine delivers it: a mechanism stated as a requirement forecloses every better one.

The cardinal rule: **ask only what the user uniquely owns.** Facts about their
business come from them; everything derivable from research comes from
research. Asking the user "who are your competitors and how are you
positioned?" is outsourcing the skill's own job — the failure mode this file
exists to prevent.

Question style (all waves):
- Plain language, zero marketing jargon. Not "what's your ICP?" but "describe
  your best current customer".
- Max 4 questions per round; multiple rounds per wave allowed but each round
  must be worth the interruption.
- Offer concrete options where a real choice exists (narrows the reply,
  speeds the round). Use the client's structured-question facility when
  available.
- **Pre-authorize "I don't know" on numeric asks.** When you ask for a figure
  the client may never have measured (conversion rate, CAC, open rate),
  append the permission in the question itself — "...ma se non l'avete mai
  misurato, va bene 'non lo so'." It removes the pressure to invent a number
  and keeps the ledger honest (an unmeasured figure becomes a researched
  BENCHMARK or a declared ASSUMPTION, never a fabricated FACT).
- Every answer that is a fact becomes a `FACT` entry in the evidence ledger,
  same discipline as researched numbers.
- **Seeds are allowed, analysis is not:** "which competitors do you lose
  deals to?" is a fact the user owns (their perceived competitive set) and a
  legitimate research seed. "What are their strengths and weaknesses?" is
  analysis — forbidden to ask, mandatory to research.

## Wave 1 — Discovery (phase 2, before any research)

Cover, across rounds:
1. **Offer** — what they sell, in their words; what problem it solves.
2. **Price & margin** — price point(s), rough margin, discount reality.
3. **Current traction** — customers today (how many, what kind), revenue
   order of magnitude, where the current customers came from.
4. **Market & language** — geography, target market, language for the
   deliverables.
5. **Budget & horizon** — marketing budget range (a range is fine), planning
   horizon (quarter / year / launch window).
6. **Team & capacity** — who will execute marketing (nobody / founder /
   person / team / agency), hours realistically available.
7. **Sales motion** — how a sale happens today (self-serve, demo calls,
   proposals, retail...), typical cycle length.
8. **Existing assets** — website, mailing list, social accounts and their
   real state; brand materials.
9. **Constraints** — legal/regulatory limits, channels they refuse, brand
   red lines.
10. **Competitor seeds** — who they lose deals to / who customers compare
    them with (seed, not analysis).

Skip path: any item already answered by an approved MKT-VISION or earlier
session record is not re-asked — note where the answer came from.

## Wave 2 — Ambition (phase 5 entry, after research)

Now the user chooses with research on the table. Present the relevant
evidence first (one short paragraph), then ask:
1. **Growth vs margin** — push volume or protect profitability?
2. **Risk appetite** — proven channels only, or reserve an experimental slice?
3. **Timeline pressure** — is there a hard date (launch, season, runway)?
4. **Non-negotiables** — anything the objectives must include or avoid?

## Wave 3 — Execution capacity (phase 7 entry)

1. **Content capacity** — who writes/records, in which language, how often
   realistically?
2. **Paid capacity** — who runs ads; any platform experience in-house?
3. **Follow-up capacity** — if leads double, who answers them? CRM/tooling?
4. **Budget flexibility** — is the budget fixed or scalable if a channel
   proves itself?

## Reflect

Fold answers into the artifacts (MKT_VISION, OBJECTIVES, TACTICAL_PLAN) and
the ledger — never leave them only in chat. The written document is what the
next session and the reviewer read.

## Anti-patterns

- **Outsourcing analysis**: asking the user for market size, competitor
  strengths, channel choice, or positioning. That is the skill's work.
- **Jargon**: ICP, CAC, SOM, funnel — translate or don't ask.
- **Interrogation**: more than 4 questions per round, or drip-feeding across
  many turns instead of structured rounds.
- **Re-asking the recorded**: anything an approved vision or ledger FACT
  already answers.
- **Collecting without folding in**: answers that never reach a document or
  the ledger are lost to the next session.
