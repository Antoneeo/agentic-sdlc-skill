## Packet 17

Locations below refer to packet-17/SLIDE_CONTENT.md sections S01–S08, normally their Learner content; plan references are explicit.

1. **Promise — sufficient.** COURSE_PLAN, “Ambito e destinatari” and “Valore atteso,” identifies new coordinators, known ticket vocabulary, unknown policy, and some users' tone-based shortcut without attributing it to everyone. “Non è disponibile una prova individuale” and operational-impact ability remaining “INCERTA” preserve uncertainty. O1–O3 promise facts-based classification, exact missing-data questions, provisional status and referral. These are useful changes within the brief rather than a topic list.

2. **Alignment — sufficient.** S05 requires “categoria, fatti decisivi e motivo per cui il tono non determina la risposta”; S07 requires a note with decision, supporting facts and a precise question or next action. S02 teaches combined conditions, S03 teaches missing versus uncovered cases, and S04 supplies a response model. These passages prepare the judgments assessed. COURSE_PLAN “Verifica e recupero” explicitly rejects an isolated label. No assessed distinction is left entirely untaught.

3. **Reasoning — sufficient.** S04's worked case has a principal function blocked for part of users, no workaround, an angry commercially important client, and a P2 conclusion: “L'assenza di una soluzione temporanea non basta a farne P1, che richiede anche il blocco per tutti.” This connects facts to a rule and rules out a plausible competing conclusion. S02 keeps the block and all-user scope fixed while changing workaround availability, explaining P1→P2. S01/S04 explicitly reject tone as an independent category determinant. Essential explanations are in learner-visible text, not merely author notes.

4. **Discrimination/transfer and fidelity — sufficient.** S05 B→C changes a decisive condition. S07 E substitutes a secondary-function block for the taught principal-function slowdown boundary; S07 F changes scope but preserves P2 because a workaround remains available. This tests whether a changed fact necessarily changes the category. S07 D lacks all three information dimensions. S08 gives correct policy-grounded actions. The policy's three categories, provisional handling, responsible-person referral and no-SLA limit are preserved; no operational definition of “principal” or “practicable” is invented.

   Plausible insufficient answers are rejected by the authored criteria and key: “D: P1, because the important customer is furious” lacks all required facts and contradicts S08-D; “E: P3 because it is not P1/P2” violates the aesthetic-only requirement, explicitly corrected in S08-E; “F becomes P1 when everyone is affected” ignores the available workaround, explicitly corrected in S08-F. “F is P2 in both cases” alone is also insufficient under S07's request for reasons and the plan's no-label-only criterion. The learner must distinguish the two P2 routes.

5. **Support and feedback — sufficient.** S01–S03 provide terms, rules and examples before attempts; S04 models a complete motivation and offers “decisione → fatti decisivi → condizione della politica,” with parallel support for unknown and uncovered cases. S06 and S08 supply authored answers and explanations. S06 asks a tone-led learner to remove client adjectives and a P1 overgeneralizer to identify the unmet condition; S08 asks the learner to separate known/unknown facts or rewrite both P2 rationales. These are corrections of specific reasoning errors rather than generic rereading. The absence of an external feedback channel does not remove this authored instructional feedback.

6. **Cognitive load — sufficient, no blocking overload found.** Terms appear in S01, combinations in S02 and uncertainty/boundaries in S03 before integration. Eight text units use tables, cases and separated solutions. S08 is substantial but has a clear D/E/F structure and a distinct correction paragraph. For a text-only self-study delivery this is manageable; no fixed-size slide fit is implied. The visible learner text contains the necessary reasoning. Complete explanation adds interpretation but is not needed to supply a missing decision rule.

7. **Recall — sufficient as design evidence only.** S05 tells readers to answer before S06; S07 precedes S08 and revisits facts, conditions and tone across new cases. There is an opportunity to reconstruct explanations and revisit concepts. No scheduled later retrieval or demonstrated retention is claimed, and none is required here.

8. **Rendering handoff — sufficient for canonical text; rendered implementation not observable.** All eight units include full learner content, text/table order, transitions and answers. Visual instructions preserve the relevant adjacency and ordering without delegating teaching to a designer. No separate rendering was provided for this packet, so actual rendering fidelity cannot be claimed.

## Packet 42

Locations refer to packet-42/SLIDE_CONTENT.md unless stated otherwise.

1. **Promise — insufficient on the full criterion, though the capability is named.** Course Value and COURSE_PLAN's alignment row promise classification with motivation and recognition of missing data for ticket-familiar novices. However, the plan offers no explicit uncertainty about the learners' unmeasured reasoning baseline. “Talvolta segue il tono” compresses the brief's “some learners” into a profile-level generalization. This is secondary to the substantive failures below; correcting this wording alone would not establish readiness.

2. **Alignment — insufficient, blocking.** S04 asks only “quali sono le tre categorie?”, which category denotes an aesthetic-only defect, what scope means, and whether tone alone changes a category. There is no ticket to classify with a justification and no information gap to identify. The plan says “risposta corretta e ragionamento conforme alla politica,” but the actual questions do not elicit that reasoning. S05's answer key accepts recall. The alignment table's claim that S01–S03 “spiegano la classificazione” does not compensate for absent application evidence.

3. **Reasoning — insufficient, blocking.** S01 restates the policy, S02 defines terms, S03 restates missing-data/referral/tone rules. No worked decision links an observed ticket's impact, scope and workaround to a conclusion. No alternative is tested against a condition; no condition is changed to show why the result changes. The tone rule is present and faithful, but no passage teaches its use in a competing emotional/operational case.

4. **Discrimination/transfer — insufficient, blocking.** There is neither a changed-condition case nor an incomplete or uncovered ticket. A learner can answer S04 correctly by reproducing short phrases from S01–S03. For example, “P1, P2, P3; P3; utenti interessati; no” meets all four actual questions and matches S05, yet supplies no evidence of either promised applied capability. A learner holding the wrong belief “any block without a workaround is P1” could give that same passing answer. The check would accept it because it never tests partial-user scope against missing workaround. Likewise, “request details” versus a precise missing-fact question is never discriminated. No invented SLA or category rule was found; fidelity of the recalled policy does not cure the absent transfer evidence.

5. **Support and feedback — insufficient for judgment, blocking.** S01–S03 offer useful reference definitions before the attempt. S05 supplies correct factual answers and targeted section pointers, such as rereading S02 if scope was wrong. This is adequate support for that small recall task. It does not provide a decision rationale or correction for the intended skill: no example explains a misclassified real-looking case, unknown workaround versus absent workaround, or why an uncovered case must go to the responsible person. No autonomous judgment attempt exists for support to lead into.

6. **Cognitive load — sufficient as presentation organization only.** Five short text units are manageable and terms are introduced. Complete explanation duplicates visible content; nothing is hidden there. The defect is missing teaching and evidence, not excessive length or a hidden prerequisite. Clear formatting cannot repair that semantic gap.

7. **Recall — sufficient as narrow design evidence.** S04 instructs answering before S05, revisiting prior definitions. This is a recall opportunity, not evidence of reasoning, transfer or durable retention. No spacing claim is made.

8. **Rendering handoff — sufficient as transcription specification, not as readiness of the course.** Full visible copy and simple reading order are supplied for all five units. A renderer can reproduce them without adding content. To deliver the promised capability, however, an author must first add teaching and checks; that pedagogical omission is captured in criteria 2–5. No actual rendering was available to verify.

## Packet 63

Locations refer to packet-63/SLIDE_CONTENT.md S01–S08, normally Learner content.

1. **Promise — sufficient.** COURSE_PLAN “Profilo e valore” retains lack of individual baseline evidence and avoids assuming all coordinators follow tone. O1–O3 give observable categories-with-reasons, precise missing-information requests/provisional treatment, and boundary/referral handling. Course Value explicitly acknowledges a policy table may already suffice for some learners.

2. **Alignment — sufficient.** The plan's O1–O3 criteria reject “P1 perché è urgente,” labels without reasons, vague “servono dettagli,” and “minore quindi P3.” S04 asks for facts and reasons with scaffolding; S06 requires decisions/actions and why, precise questions when information is missing and referral when appropriate. S01 teaches the dimensions, S02 their combinations, and S03 uncertainty versus uncovered facts. All assessed decisions have an explanatory preparation.

3. **Reasoning — sufficient.** S02's export example explicitly declares principal function, all users and no practical workaround, then says the reason is that the conditions occur “insieme.” It independently varies workaround and scope to explain two routes to P2. S01 explains why an angry export complaint leaves information gaps; S03 rejects tone/commercial importance as independent determinants. S05-A fully explains partial scope/no workaround/angry customer→P2 and why P1 fails. The combination gives novice readers a visible connection between observations, rule, conclusion and a plausible error before the independent task.

4. **Discrimination/transfer and fidelity — sufficient.** S06-A asks for a scope-change prediction. S06-B uses incorrect labels whose operational consequences are unknown; the word “etichette” is not enough for P3. S06-C changes the taught uncovered-case boundary to an explicitly non-principal function block, requiring referral despite complete facts. S06-D pits anger/commercial importance/all-user spread against an explicitly aesthetic-only defect. S03/S08 preserve the policy's provisional rule, referral and absence of SLA. S01 explicitly refuses to invent criteria for principal-function status or practicability.

   Plausible insufficient answer: “B: P3, because labels are cosmetic.” S07-B rejects it: “‘Etichette sbagliate’ non dimostra che il difetto sia soltanto estetico,” and requires the three missing dimensions plus provisional classification. “C: P2, because only some users are affected” is rejected in S07-C because principal-function status is also required. “A: P1” without a reason fails the explicit S06/plan criterion even if the label is correct. S04-B's “P2 because an alternative has been proposed” is also rejected in S05: proposal is not confirmed practicability. These checks separate correct-looking labels from warranted reasoning.

5. **Support and feedback — sufficient.** S02/S03 work examples before S04's partial response structure. S05 authors the rationale and asks a P1 overgeneralizer to rewrite the reason including scope. For an assumed workaround it asks a precise practicability question. S06 removes prefilled fields; S07 maps errors to omitted conditions and specific repairs, including naming the three gaps if “more information” was too vague. Support fades within the course and corrective feedback is usable without a teacher.

6. **Cognitive load — sufficient, no blocking overload found.** S01 introduces terms and their definitional limits before use. S02 separates the initial case and independent variations with “Ripartiamo”; S03 has two numbered situations. S04/S06 separate cases, with later solution units. S07 has four substantial responses but clear case labels and nearby reasons/corrections; that is acceptable in the requested text medium. Required reasoning is learner-visible and not dependent on Complete explanation or a teacher.

7. **Recall — sufficient as design evidence.** S04 and S06 explicitly stop the reader before solutions. Concepts recur in less guided tasks. S08 suggests later reconstructing why A changes category and C requires referral, then comparing S07, while stating a successful attempt today does not establish retention. This is a suggestion, not autonomous scheduling or a claim of measured spacing effects.

8. **Rendering handoff — sufficient for text; see comparison below.** Full copy, sequencing, cases, criteria and corrective feedback are canonical. No illustration requires invented content. Complete explanation adds commentary but none is necessary to understand the declared learner packet.
