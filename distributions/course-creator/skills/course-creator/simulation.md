# Independent learner simulation

Read before L3 production to assign roles; run the diagnostic after a course draft exists. It can reveal defects in the explanation; human learning requires separate evidence. A green structural validator cannot substitute for it.

## Author, readers and didactic reviewer

For L3 production, the principal agent coordinates the following roles using the client's independent-session facilities under `review.md`. This realizes the existing content review and learner test, not an additional review gate. L1 keeps its local check; L2 verification stays proportional to the affected material.

| Role | Receives | Produces and owns |
|---|---|---|
| Principal author/coordinator | Approved Vision, profiles, objectives, sources, concept graph, plan and complete text | Owns the learning path and is the sole integrator of canonical artifacts; prepares packets, dispatches sessions and applies corrections. |
| Independent reader, one fresh session per tested profile | That profile's established prerequisites and only the learner units revealed so far | Returns interpretations and difficulties before each next reveal, then attempts the frozen task; writes no course content and does not issue a readiness verdict. |
| Independent didactic reviewer, who did not author the material | Approved Vision, profiles/objectives, graph, plan, source text and sources, frozen criteria/key, preserved reader and control responses with version/access limits | Locates defects against learning_design.md, assesses the attempts and returns a scoped verdict and findings for the author. This is the existing content/closure reviewer. |

Use distinct contexts: the reviewer receives the full evidence, so cannot also serve as the sequential reader. Readers for different profiles do not receive one another's responses. For the final comparison, each tested profile additionally needs the separate no-course control below; three core roles do not mean exactly three sessions. Run sessions in batches when concurrency is limited, preserving their separate histories and material access.

The reviewer returns the failing passage, the unmet criterion and the evidence. The principal revises the canonical source and affected graph/plan links, records the new version, and dispatches a fresh reader for the affected prefix. Preserve old findings and responses. The reviewer rechecks the correction under `review.md`'s existing round limit; a learner saying “understood” is not a substitute for that judgement. Record role/session identities and packet versions in SIMULATION_REPORT and the reviewer verdict in REVIEW_LOG.

Do not default to one author per slide or module. If parallel drafting is chosen for a large course, each bounded draft receives its assigned units, sources and expected learner knowledge at entry and exit. The principal integrates the drafts and verifies the full sequence. `dispatch.md` retains its governed-artifact boundary and optional production PLAN workflow; independent readers/reviewers do not require an extra dispatch PLAN. If independent facilities are unavailable, record the learner test as not run and follow `review.md` for a truthfully labelled review fallback. Never present the principal's role-play as an independent reader or reviewer.

## Protocol, fixed before testing

For each learner profile, freeze the course version or material hash, objective, expected evidence of comprehension, prompts/tasks, tools and permitted sources. Construct two independent fresh sessions with the **same** learner profile, final tasks, tools and external access. The course session receives the learner packet incrementally as described below; the control receives no course or reading dialogue. Neither gets project design discussion, answer key, the other's output or hidden facts the real learner would lack. Repository/KB access is allowed only when it matches the real learner context and cannot expose unrevealed course material or expected answers. Otherwise remove it from both sessions or mark the comparison contaminated. When the course teaches work with a tool, agent or delegated process that performs operations for the learner (`learning_design.md` §1), the simulated learner is itself an agent and can answer well by performing the tool's operations. Place it in the person's role as the approved course Vision describes, and give the final task a tool output to judge or a request to formulate wherever the real situation has one.

## Sequential reading: withhold what comes next

Deliver one learner-visible unit at a time, in the intended reading order. The learner keeps the prefix already read but receives no future slides, complete source, graph, author notes or solutions to unanswered checks. Guided delivery includes only the narration authored for that point. Start a fresh learner when earlier exposure has revealed future content. Restrict tools and files where the client permits; if withholding is enforced only by instructions, record that limitation rather than claiming technical isolation.

Before revealing the next unit, preserve a short response: what the learner now thinks the course or current issue is about, what this unit adds, and any reference or inference they cannot follow from the available prefix. On the opening, check whether the situation and promised usefulness are intelligible; do not demand mastery of the course. Keep the prompt neutral and give no explanations or corrective hints during the run. An understandable question awaiting its answer is not a failure by itself.

The independent didactic reviewer compares those responses with learning_design.md §3 and the graph-to-passage trace. Record the first missing prerequisite, unclear referent or broken connection at its unit. Later material does not erase that finding or retroactively validate the earlier unit. The principal corrects the earliest affected explanation or order and retests the affected prefix with a fresh reader. Preserve responses before each reveal in the existing SIMULATION_REPORT evidence; do not rewrite them with hindsight. A sampled prefix diagnoses only that prefix: unread units remain unverified.

Keep the final transfer task and no-course control as a separate comparison. Intermediate reflection prompts can themselves support understanding, so disclose them when interpreting a final score; the reading diagnostic does not establish unprompted learning or a causal course benefit. The author may inspect the evidence to revise the material; the independent reviewer owns its acceptance judgement.

After reading, ask the course learner to apply what it understood to the frozen task and cite passages it used. Preserve verbatim prompts and responses, material version, session identifiers, tool permissions and access. The independent didactic reviewer evaluates both final attempts against the criteria fixed earlier; the simulator does not grade itself. If the control already succeeds, the course-assisted success is inconclusive. If a learner blocks, misses a prerequisite or carries away a wrong fact, the principal corrects the **explanation** and retests with fresh sessions.

Use `SIMULATION_REPORT.md` to record the two attempts, comparison, limitations and revision. If independent sessions are unavailable, write `Status: not run` and the reason; an author self-pass is not an independent learner. A diagnostic PASS never changes `COURSE_PLAN.md` from `efficacy not verified`.

## Actual learner packet and value

Build the packet from the canonical source in `slide_content.md`: learner-visible copy, captions and transitions for self-study; also authored narration for guided delivery. Exclude solutions and answer keys from both attempts. Freeze a new-case transfer task that tests the declared change in understanding or decision, not recall of labels. Identify the explanation expected to enable it. A definition-only course with no useful changed judgement fails semantic review; model success alone, especially a successful control, cannot establish added learning.

## Freeze evidence and distinguish what the test evaluates

Use the objective-specific criteria from `learning_design.md`, including a plausible insufficient response and the explanation expected to correct it. Do not leak that response, the key or the desired reasoning into the learner prompt. Fix criteria before seeing results. Record whether the task asks recall, understanding, application or transfer; recalling the course's named steps cannot establish a promised decision skill. For a course on a tool, agent or delegated process that performs operations for the learner (see Protocol above), the frozen criteria score the person's acts (the request, the judgement of the tool's output, the reserved decision) and give no credit for producing, in any form, an output the tool produces for the person.

For evaluations of course-creator itself, use fresh producer sessions with a realistic user brief and raw sources. Do not restate the teaching techniques being tested in that brief. Keep baseline/revision on the same request, source access and model settings, preserve both outputs and have an independent reviewer apply frozen criteria without version labels. Include a structurally complete but pedagogically inadequate fixture when testing the review gate. A positive result is scoped to the observed runs; a successful baseline makes incremental benefit inconclusive. A production comparison is not a learner/control simulation and neither proves human learning.
